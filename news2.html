Ghana's 2026 Mid-Year Budget Review, Explained

On Thursday, July 23, 2026, Finance Minister Dr. Cassiel Ato Forson presented the 2026 Mid-Year Budget Review to Parliament — a legally required update on how the economy has performed in the first half of the year, and what the government plans for the rest of it.

1. The Headline Numbers

  • Inflation: down to 5.3%, a significant drop since November 2025.
  • Interest rates: Bank of Ghana held its key lending rate steady at 14%.
  • No supplementary budget: government will realign existing spending instead.
  • Primary fiscal surplus: expected at about 2.6% of GDP.

2. The Currency and Debt Picture

The cedi has depreciated against the US dollar over the past year, trading at roughly GH¢11.52 to $1. Minority MPs point out this depreciation increases Ghana's debt when measured in local currency. There have also been calls for the Finance Ministry to reconcile differing debt-to-GDP figures published by the Bank of Ghana and the IMF.

3. What the Government Is Saying

Dr. Forson framed the improved indicators as the result of sacrifices made by ordinary Ghanaians, describing the recovery as moving "from the emergency room to the wellness centre," and stating the gains belong to the people of Ghana, not the government.

4. What the Opposition Is Saying

The Minority argues the improved figures reflect reduced spending rather than genuine growth. Minority Leader Alexander Afenyo-Markin questioned spending priorities, such as market expansion projects while hospital development lags. Other critics called the reported 3% growth rate underwhelming compared to past years.

5. Why This Matters Going Forward

Parliament's debate continues over the following days. The review also signals how Ghana plans to manage its transition away from its current IMF programme later this year.
